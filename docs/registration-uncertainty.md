`models/Estimation/Localization/RGBDRegistrationUncertainty.mo` computes conditional, first-order noise covariance for the existing **unweighted** rigid point fit. It does not change correspondence selection or the fit. It accepts calibrated matched optical RDF points, exact enabled masks, an accepted source→target transform, retained estimated reference-body rotation and camera extrinsics. It has no truth input. `capacity=350` retains every frontend slot, including sparse late matches; the structural parameter can also be set to 14400. Admission at one capacity does not establish admission at another.

The ordered `ObserveRGBDRelativeFrame` frontend now defaults to bounded
consensus when the original fit refuses its RMS residual. The final fit remains
unweighted, with the same 0.02 m default RMS bound. Its certified sparse inlier
mask supplies both registration uncertainty and displayed tracking; descriptor
candidate count remains separate from accepted inlier count. Clean accepted
fits retain the original result. `robustRegistration=false` selects the original
unweighted baseline, also retained by the equation-model frontend. Covariance
is conditional on the selected inliers: consensus selection bias and uncertain
correspondence identity are still outside this first-order model. See the
[rendered-city reference evidence](../dev/artifacts/modelica-rendered-city-slam/README.md).

For a reference point p and fitted rotation R, the perturbation is `δy = δt - skew(R p) δθ`; rotation is a left optical perturbation. With `J=[I,-skew(R p)]`, the component accumulates `H=Σ JᵀJ` and `M=Σ Jᵀ(C_current + R C_reference Rᵀ)J` in source slot order. It computes the sandwich `H⁻¹ M H⁻ᵀ`. Inverting weighted information would describe a different estimator. This is a local linearization with fixed correspondences, not the exact nonlinear covariance at arbitrary residuals. The treatment of measurement covariance and limitations of registration uncertainty follow [Censi’s original derivation](https://censi.science/pub/research/2007-icra-icpcov.pdf); [Brossard, Bonnabel and Barrau](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05722) discuss the overconfidence caused by unmodeled systematic and correlated errors.

The optical point model uses RGB pixel localization sigma and disparity sigma at the **physical depth noise-reference focal length**, separately for reference and current frames. For axial depth z, `σ_z = z² σ_d/(noiseReferenceFx*baseline)`. RGB focal lengths govern the lateral pixel derivatives; reduced depth-image fx must not replace noiseReferenceFx. The covariance includes the off-diagonal terms from the common depth error. The named noise defaults are tunable hypotheses, not a manufacturer guarantee. RealSense documents the stereo disparity geometry and systematic subpixel/calibration effects in its [D400 subpixel-linearity white paper](https://dev.realsenseai.com/docs/white-paper-subpixel-linearity-improvement-for-intel-realsense-depth-cameras/).

The descriptor frontend interpolates inverse depth. For nonnegative normalized bilinear weights, inverse-depth disparity sensitivity is constant; the sum of weighted standard deviations is a conservative bound even when the four neighbors are fully correlated. This component gives **no four-neighbor averaging discount**: it uses that common disparity bound, with editable `depthInflation>=1`. This covers neighbor noise correlation within the interpolation footprint under the stated equal disparity-noise bound. It does not cover arbitrary depth gradients, occlusions, interpolation bias, calibration bias, or localization-induced resampling of an unknown discontinuity. Frontend discontinuity/valid-neighbor checks remain required. Pixel localization and disparity are modeled independently, as are the two frames and distinct correspondence pairs. Reused/shared pixels or temporal filtering can violate those assumptions; inflation alone is not a proof that omitted cross-pair terms are bounded.

The output `relativeCovariance` is ordered `[optical δt, optical left δθ]`. The additional `observationCovariance` is conditional on the retained reference pose and ordered `[world additive position, body right-local angle]`, matching `ES15PoseCorrection`. Let `E=opticalToBody`, `o=cameraOriginBody`, `C=referenceBodyRotation*E*Rᵀ`, and `a=t+Eᵀo`. The exact first-order transport Jacobian is

```
G = [-C   -C*skew(a)]
    [ 0          -E]
```

The lever arm contributes to position uncertainty even during a pure rotation. The independent test differentiates the complete `RGBDRelativePose` composition under a general 6DOF rotation and nonidentity extrinsic, rather than differentiating a duplicate Jacobian expression.

`observationCovariance` is **not an independent absolute measurement covariance** when the reference pose came from the same filter. Reference-pose uncertainty, cross-covariance with the current state, correspondence selection, robust selection bias and dynamic objects are not included. An augmented reference-state filter should consume `relativeCovariance` and preserve that correlation. Directly feeding this conditional transported covariance to an absolute-pose update would risk double counting retained information. This component alone does not complete visual SLAM.

The Modelica function rejects malformed counts/masks, nonfinite active points or calibration, nonpositive axial depth, rejected registration, improper rotations, insufficient points, singular/ill-conditioned normal matrices, or a non-SPD result. Disabled NaN points never enter accumulation. Rejection publishes **zero covariance with `valid=0`**, never a usable zero-noise observation. Reasons: 1 configuration; 2 pose/registration; 3 active pair; 4 insufficient; 5 normal rank/conditioning; 6 output SPD. The dimensionless, diagonally equilibrated Cholesky pivot gate is tunable; no damping or covariance floor is inserted. Planar noncollinear points can be admissible; collinear/coincident sets must refuse.
